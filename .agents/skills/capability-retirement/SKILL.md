# Capability retirement

Use when a capability, implementation, public contract, provider, policy or authoritative information source is replaced or withdrawn, or temporary compatibility is introduced or ended. This skill does not authorize destruction or scope expansion.

1. Establish the destination, replacement authority, preserved contracts, explicitly retired behavior and retention/security obligations. Distinguish replacement from additive support and current authority from historical evidence. Surface consequential unresolved disposition rather than assuming indefinite compatibility or destructive removal.
2. Inventory implementations, callers, exports, registrations, routes, configuration, flags, dependencies, permissions, credentials, deployment units, tests, docs, stores, caches, indexes, replicas, jobs, backups and recovery paths. Trace across repositories and deployed versions as relevant; distinguish evidence from unknowns.
3. Assign each superseded element a disposition: retain as supported, maintain through bounded transition, archive with restricted access, or remove. Record owner, reason, dependencies and acceptance evidence. Temporary compatibility requires actual consumers, maintenance/security obligations, parity checks, exit date or milestone, tracked removal task and accountable owner. If compatibility is explicitly rejected, do not introduce it.
4. Plan dependency order, data integrity, rollout and recovery. Replace implementations behind existing valid domain contracts rather than adding parallel dispatchers, interception wrappers, fallback authority or dual writes. Temporary bridging requires explicit bounded justification. Establish authorization, retention, migration/backup evidence and recovery behavior before removing access or deleting data. If retirement forbids old-data reads, use replacement-side evidence and report missing data as a blocker rather than reopening the old source. Recovery must respect new authority.
5. Execute authorized changes: update consumers; remove obsolete paths, registrations, exports, dependencies, flags, credentials/grants, jobs and operational access as applicable. Invalidate or rebuild derived data; exclude expired information from current lookup and decision paths. Preserve intentionally supported historical reads, identities and provenance without treating them as current truth. Remove obsolete code/tests while preserving domain behavior coverage. Mark historical docs and update active instructions. Physical data deletion is a separate authorized disposition.
6. Verify positive and negative contracts. Test replacement success and failures; use disposable absent, inaccessible, stale or conflicting old-data/configuration fixtures when appropriate. Check no fallback, hidden probes, retired discovery, jobs or dependency reachability; verify deployed registrations, permissions, consumers and restart/recovery as relevant. Prove expired information cannot influence current behavior through caches, indexes or defaults. Verify retained historical access or compatibility separately. Distinguish source removal, deployment and operational verification.
7. Report inventory, disposition, owner, evidence, outstanding obligations and blockers. Mark retirement verified, unverified or blocked; distinguish temporary support from completed removal. Do not declare retirement complete on passing replacement tests alone.

Coordinate verification and reporting with the enclosing migration or feature. Reuse valid evidence and batch checks at coherent boundaries; retain explicit irreversible cutover, safety and release gates. Do not turn inventory or implementation milestones into independent full-suite or user approval gates.

See [lifecycle rationale](../../../docs/CAPABILITY_LIFECYCLE.md) and [acceptance cases](../../../docs/CAPABILITY_RETIREMENT_CASES.md). Coordinate requirements with requirements-review, boundary decisions with architecture-review, restructuring with refactoring and evidence with change-verification. Apply proportionally; do not invent a retirement framework unless the project requires one.
